## 2026-10-25 02:05 (+02:00), before the complete BS1 extraction, the BS1 analysis and BS2 (none has been run on the complete data)

No deviation from the pre-registration. Facts that exist before this entry: the BS0 catalogue and latency (`results/v2/BS0`), unit tests on synthetic data, and a geometry check of the code on three cached winter acquisitions (2023-11-15, 2023-12-05, 2026-01-03; sun elevation 29-35 degrees; scene cloud 0%): median RSS(d_hat) / RSS(constant) over 701 tank fits is 0.153 with the physical sun direction and 0.998 with the mirrored direction (physical lower in 86% of tanks); registration shifts 0.6-3.7 m, none at the search edge. This is a check of the code orientation, not a test outcome. No EIA value has been compared with any satellite quantity.

Implementation choices that the pre-registration leaves open, fixed now:

1. **Collection and de-duplication.** Both collections were paged to the end and filtered to items whose footprint contains the window: `sentinel-2-l2a` 1,626 items, 597 distinct dates, 603 (date, platform, relative orbit) acquisitions; `sentinel-2-c1-l2a` 1,036 items, 509 dates, 515 acquisitions. `sentinel-2-l2a` is chosen (more distinct dates). The relative orbit is read from the product name (R112 for every acquisition). Among several items of one acquisition: EPSG:32614 first, then the footprint whose edge is farthest from the window, then the newest processing baseline, then the highest item id. The window lies in the overlap of tiles 14SPE and 14SQE, so 602 of 603 acquisitions come from 14SPE. Two platforms flying 27 s apart on the same date (S2A and S2C in November-December 2024, S2A and S2B in February 2026) are two acquisitions, since the rule keys on the platform.
2. **Sentinel-1.** Planetary Computer items are grouped by (date, platform in lower case, orbit state, relative orbit); a group covers the window if the union of its footprints contains it; availability uses covering groups only.
3. **Windows read.** The WGS84 window is transformed to the raster CRS, grown by 40 m and snapped outward to whole 10 m pixels; the pad is clipped at the raster edge (the one acquisition taken from tile 14SQE loses one pixel column of the pad on the west side). SCL (20 m) is repeated 2 x 2 onto the 10 m pixels of the same window.
4. **Registration.** Pearson correlation between B04 and the synthetic image (all 333 registry disks, coverage fraction on a 1 m sub-grid, summed and capped at 1), over the central pixels leaving a 2-pixel margin (identical pixels for every shift); integer shifts of -2..+2 pixels in each axis; each axis refined by a parabola through the peak and its two neighbours; an axis whose peak lies on the search border is not refined and the acquisition is flagged. No cloud mask is applied. The translation is added to the projected registry centres of every tank.
5. **Pixel model.** As written (1 m sub-grid, interior pixel if at least 90% of its 100 sub-points are inside the disk, f_p = sub-points inside the disk and in shadow / 100, depths 0..20 m in 0.5 m steps). A pixel with B04 = 0 (no data) inside a disk makes the tank few_pixels.
6. **Fit.** For each depth the least-squares fit of a and b with b <= a; if the unconstrained solution has b > a, or the lit and shadow columns cannot be separated (relative determinant below 1e-6), the constant fit a = b = mean is used. b has no other bound. d_hat minimises RSS over the grid; exact ties (identical shadow columns on the 1 m sub-grid, or a plateau when one pixel carries the shadow) take the midpoint of the first run of tied depths; if no depth lowers RSS below the constant fit, d_hat = 0. The 68% interval is the hull of {d: RSS(d) - RSS_min <= sigma2_hat}.
7. **Abstention order and kept estimates.** few_pixels (fewer than 8 interior pixels), then cloudy (any SCL class 3, 8, 9, 10, 11 on the disk plus 20 m, at the registered centre), then no_contrast (d_hat > 0 and a - b < 3 sigma_hat), then full_or_fixed (d_hat = 0, kept), then wide_interval (hull wider than 6 m, kept), else ok. "Identified d" in BS2 means status ok, wide_interval or full_or_fixed.
8. **Clear acquisition** (weekly proxy of BS2 and availability of BS0): at least 50% of the summed disk area of the tanks with diameter >= 40 m carries the tank-level clear flag. Availability: acquisition time + 24 h <= Tuesday 16:30 ET (API) or Wednesday 10:30 ET (EIA) of the week after F; window +-2 days of Friday 07:00 America/Chicago.
9. **Identifiability simulation (ILL).** By sun-elevation bin (25, 35, 45, 55, 65 degrees edges), 30 catalogue acquisitions x 40 registry tanks x depths 0, 2, ..., 14 m, seed 20261025. The fit assumes the nominal centre on a random pixel phase; the truth is rendered with the centre displaced by up to +-5 m in each axis. Lit-roof level = median a and contrast = median (a - b) of the real fits with status ok or wide_interval in the bin; noise sigma drawn from the residual sigmas of the real fits of the bin; ground outside the disk equals the shadow level. The contrast comes from identified fits only and therefore flatters the simulation.
10. **Stability.** Pairs of acquisitions at most 2 days apart with an estimate for the tank (including the 27 s pairs), robust spread = 1.4826 x MAD of the d_hat differences, pooled and per tank (tanks with at least 3 pairs), by sun-elevation bin of the pair mean, split by gap (same pass under 1 h, or later).
11. **BS2.** One acquisition per week (closest to Friday 07:00 CT within +-2 days, ties: earlier). Delta V = -sum A_i (d_F - d_{F-1}) over tanks identified at both dates, A_i = pi (diameter / 2)^2, x 6.2898 bbl per m3, in kbbl, compared with the first-published change in kbbl. Primary sample: both acquisitions at sun elevation <= 45 degrees and identified tanks (at both dates) covering at least 50% of the disk area of all 234 tanks. The verdict uses the Fisher-z 95% interval; the moving-block bootstrap (blocks of 4 consecutive calendar weeks over 2018-12-07..2026-10-16, 5,000 draws, seed 20261025, percentile 95%, a fresh generator per statistic) is reported beside it, with a flag if the two verdicts differ. If both "lower bound > 0" and "upper bound < 0.20" hold, SIGNAL (listed first in the rule); fewer than 5 weeks: INCONCLUSIVE.
12. **Nowcast.** M0: AR(2) with intercept, expanding window on all first-published changes since 2011-07-22, at least 104 regression rows before the first forecast. beta: OLS without intercept of earlier M0 errors on Delta V over earlier valid weeks (at least 20). Evaluated on the primary-sample weeks; 95% percentile interval of the paired-loss difference (moving blocks of 4 weeks, 5,000 draws); GAIN if its upper end is below 0, otherwise NO GAIN DETECTED.
13. **Single run.** `scripts/bs_run_all.ps1` deletes any previous `results/v2/BS2/bs2.json` and runs `scripts/bs2_aggregate.py` once, after the extraction has finished without failed acquisitions; each step is logged in `results/v2/trial_log_v2.csv`.

## 2026-10-25 02:29 (+02:00), before T1c, T2c and B0 are computed (no outcome statistic of these three tests exists yet)

No deviation from the pre-registration. Facts that exist before this entry: the built-in check `scripts/t1c_t2c_calendar.py --calendar rule` reproduces the WTI blocks of `results/T1.json` and `results/T2.json` (44 fields with 41 numbers and 35 fields with 31 numbers; maximum relative difference 0); the calendar facts of items 1-2 (release dates only, no return); the availability counts of `scripts/b0_transmission.py --dry` (event days, presence of minute bars and of first-release rows: no price difference, no clock-check share, no surprise value, no regression); a check of the loader clock with the CME daily halt (the New York hour 17 is the emptiest hour of Monday-Thursday in every year and every clock regime, 2013-2023 and 2026; the Sunday session opens at 18:00 New York on 532 of 547 Sundays). New code: `src/altdata/histdata.py` (loader, self-test of eleven known clock conversions), `scripts/t1c_t2c_calendar.py`, `scripts/b0_transmission.py`, `scripts/fig_b0.py`.

Implementation choices that the pre-registration leaves open, fixed now:

**T1c / T2c**

1. **Archive calendar.** Rows of `audit/eia_release_registry.csv` whose note contains "data-only folder" are dropped; the others are keyed by `week_ending_friday` (exact match with the Friday of the EIA stocks index; the earliest `release_date` if a week has several rows). A week without a row keeps its v1 rule date, and `is_trading_day` is recomputed on the WTI trading days as in v1. 790 weeks have an archive row and 15 of them change release day: 12 with the rule or the archive date inside 2010-01-04..2024-04-05 (this is `n_weeks_redated` in the outputs; 11 move by one day and 2013-10-11 by four days) and three after the sample (2024-12-20, 2025-12-19, 2026-06-19). Five weeks after 2011-07-22 whose rule date lies in the sample keep it: 2016-08-05, 2017-02-03 and 2017-02-17 (their registry rows carry a non-Friday week ending read from the report page, 2016-08-03, 2017-02-01 and 2017-02-23, so no Friday matches; the rule date equals the archive folder date in all three) and 2022-06-17 and 2023-11-03 (data-only folders, ignored as pre-registered; their rule dates 2022-06-23 and 2023-11-08 are days without a report, the data came out on 2022-06-29 and 2023-11-15). Natural gas is not recomputed: the NG release days of the WTI placebo in T1c are the v1 ones.
2. **Reproduction check and single run.** Every number of the recomputed block must equal the saved block within a relative difference of 1e-12 (|a - b| / max(|a|, |b|)), every string must be identical and the sets of fields must be equal; the archive run repeats the check first and stops on any difference. The script refuses to write if `results/v2/T1c.json` or `T2c.json` exists. The two outputs keep the layout of `T1.json` and `T2.json` (`markets.WTI`) and add `n_weeks_redated`, `n_weeks_redated_all_years`, `redated_weeks`, `weeks_with_rule_date_kept_after_archive_start`, the check result and the v1 reference values.

**B0**

3. **Event days.** The registry rows without the data-only note are dated by the archive folder in `source_url`, not by the page date in `release_date`; the two are identical except in three rows where the page header is wrong: folder 2017_02_23 (page 2017-03-01; a Thursday release after Presidents' Day), 2026_06_24 (page 2026-06-14, a Sunday) and 2026_10_07 (page 2026-09-30, the date of the previous release). Candidate events are the rows dated 2013-08-01..2023-12-31 or 2026-06-01..2026-10-24 (both ends inclusive): 562. The weekday is taken from the folder date.
4. **Exclusions, in this order; each event is counted once, under its first reason (events.csv also carries one flag per reason).** (1) holiday shifted: the archive day is not a Wednesday (81); (2) data-only day: a Wednesday on which a data-only folder was released with the regular report (2022-06-29, 2023-11-15), because two data weeks were first published together and the surprise of the day is not the one of a single week (2); (3) other release time: a Wednesday listed with a time other than 10:30 a.m. in `audit/eia_release_times.csv` (the file lists no Wednesday: 0); (4) no minute data: no WTI bar starts between 10:00 and 10:59 ET that day (15); (5) missing minute: no bar starting at 10:29 or at 10:35 (0 after (4)); the 11:00 bar is required only by the secondary outcome, an event without it stays in the primary sample; (6) no surprise: no first-release row for the folder date, or a lag or the history missing (3). 461 events remain and 464 (those not excluded by (1)-(5)) enter the clock check.
5. **Minute convention.** "Close at HH:MM" is the bid close of the bar that starts at HH:MM ET, so dP = P(10:36:00) - P(10:30:00) and the secondary outcome is P(11:01:00) - P(10:30:00). Loader: rule AM1 as in `intraday_momentum_gate0.load_minutes` (New York local time before 2019-01-01, Europe/Athens minus 7 hours from 2019-01-01; repeated or missing local times become NaT and are dropped); all WTIUSD zips are read (annual 2013-2023, monthly 2026-06 to 2026-10; no file exists for 2024-01..2026-05; the data end on 2023-12-01 and 2026-10-16, and the 2026-06 file starts on 2026-06-28). Bars that share one instant (identical copies of the repeated Sunday hour in 2019-2023; late single-tick bars in the 2026-08..10 files) are reduced to the one with the widest high-low range, ties to the later row of the file (gate0 keeps the later row only; it differs when the later bar is a single tick). No minute is filled.
6. **Clock check.** Sample: the events not excluded by (1)-(5) (reason (6) is not applied: the clock does not depend on the surprise). For each event, the change of the minute starting at m is close(m) - close(m - 1 minute), defined when both bars exist, for m = 10:00..10:59; the argmax of its absolute value (the earliest if tied) is recorded. Share = events whose argmax is 10:30 / events in the sample; the threshold is 0.70, inclusive. Below it, B0 is BLOCKED: `events.csv` (availability columns), `b0.json`, `sanity_clock.json` and the trial-log line are written and the dP values and the regression are not computed. Reported beside the share (descriptive, not part of the rule): the same share on all event days with any bar between 10:00 and 10:59 that are not excluded by (1)-(3), the shares by year and by clock regime, the distribution of the argmax minute, and the mean absolute one-minute change at 10:15..10:45 (the left panel of the figure comes from this sample).
7. **Surprises.** `eia_first_release.csv` is sorted by (week_ending, release_date) and the first row of each week kept (this drops the 2019-07-03 table, which repeats the week ending 2019-06-21, and leaves the week ending 2019-06-28 without a first-published value). Each series sits on the Friday grid at 7-day steps; the weekly change is (current - prior printed in the same release) / 1000, in million barrels; US commercial crude excluding Cushing is (us_comm_crude - cushing) in both terms. AR(2): for week t, least squares of the change on a constant and its two lags over the weeks s < t whose change and two lags exist, at least 104 such rows; the forecast needs the two lags of t; surprise = change - forecast. The event takes the week of the archive folder whose date equals the event day (the `release_date` of the first-release file carries the same page-header errors as the registry, so the join uses the folder date from `source_url`). Weeks 2019-07-10 and 2019-07-17 lack a lag (the 2019-06-28 gap) and 2019-07-03 has no first-release row: the three reason-(6) exclusions.
8. **Model.** statsmodels OLS with `cov_type="HC1"`; intervals and p-values use the normal approximation (the statsmodels default for robust covariances); coefficients and standard errors are multiplied by 100 (cents per barrel per million barrels). The verdict uses the upper end of the 95% interval of the Cushing coefficient (below 0: TRANSMISSION DETECTED; otherwise NOT DETECTED). The secondary outcome uses the same model on the events that have a 11:00 bar, without verdict. No outlier rule and no winsorisation; the five largest |dP| are listed descriptively in `b0.json`.
9. **Single run and trial log.** `scripts/b0_transmission.py` refuses to run if `results/v2/B0/b0.json` exists and writes it last. `results/v2/trial_log_v2.csv` was created by the BS driver with six columns (id, timestamp, script, prereg_v2_sha256, verdict, seconds); the lines of T1c, T2c and B0 are appended to it with the same six columns and the note in brackets after the verdict, so that the file stays rectangular.

## 2026-10-25 02:40 (+01:00), amendment AM-B0-1, written after the B0 clock check and before any B0 outcome statistic

Known at this point: the pre-registered clock check ran once and failed (the 10:30 minute is the largest one-minute move of 10:00-10:59 ET on 205 of 464 event days, 44.2% < 70%; argmax shares by year 28-63% in 2013-2023 and 0 of 14 in 2026); the pooled event-day profile of the mean absolute one-minute change peaks at the 10:30 minute (24.5 cents) against 3.1-4.0 cents for 10:15-10:29. That result stays on record in results/v2/B0 (BLOCKED). No price difference around the print, no surprise value and no regression has been computed.

Reason: the argmax rule asks that the single largest minute be 10:30. With a correct clock it still fails when volatility stays high for several minutes after the print, so it does not test the clock. It was a badly specified validity check.

Amended gate (validity only, no outcome): for each calendar year of event days, the mean absolute one-minute change over 10:15-10:45 ET must peak at the 10:30 minute, and its 10:30 value must exceed 3 times its mean over 10:15-10:29. Event days of a failing year are excluded. If fewer than 200 events remain, the test is BLOCKED. Everything else is as pre-registered (outcome, surprises, model, verdict rule).

Status: B0 is reported as EXPLORATORY (validity gate amended after its first run). Output: results/v2/B0_amended (run once, script flag --amended); the first run is not overwritten.
