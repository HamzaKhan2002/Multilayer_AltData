1Executive summary

Objective. Build, within a six-month internship, one systematic strategy whose edge comes from information the market has not yet priced, and prove it at a level that justifies capital. The system is asset-class independent: sources, measurement and regimes change per idea; point-in-time discipline, validation and risk do not. Six ideas share it; I1 is developed first because its data can be tested now on free sources.

State of the work on 25 October 2026.

  • Implemented A Python toolkit (EIA files and report archive, trading calendars, statistics, Sentinel-1/-2 access, OpenStreetMap tank registry, ENTSO-E, GIE AGSI+, Open-Meteo, HistData minute loader, roof-shadow model), 33 unit tests on synthetic data, two frozen pre-registrations whose hashes every test script verifies, single-run guards and trial logs.
  • Empirical finding EIA report days are about 10% more volatile for WTI (1.10; 1.11 with the true release dates) and natural gas (1.09), less clearly since 2017. A naive surprise-direction test is not supported. Sentinel-2 roof-shadow depths are repeatable per tank; the weekly hub-level test is inconclusive. At the print, US ex-Cushing, gasoline and distillate surprises are priced within six minutes Exploratory; the Cushing coefficient is not detected on the outright.
  • Proposed The nowcast ladder B1, the price-information test B2, the net-value test B3, the replication A, a vendor-data evaluation protocol, and a six-month plan with three gates whose outcomes are promising, inconclusive or stop.
Scientific reading so far. The free 10 m optical route measures floating-roof depth repeatably, but at Cushing its weekly cadence (one orbit, clouds, sun angle) is the binding constraint. The outright WTI price absorbs inventory news within minutes, and the Cushing-specific part is small on the outright. Value, if any, must come from (i) incorporation before the API and EIA releases, (ii) the CL1–CL2 spread, where hub scarcity should show, or (iii) higher-cadence data. Each is tested before money is spent (Sections 5–9).
Statistical reality. A weekly event strategy makes about 52 decisions a year. The standard error of a forecast–price correlation measured on n decisions is about 1/√n: telling ρ = 0.06 from zero at two standard errors needs about 1,100 decisions, two decades of reports. PnL alone cannot validate I1 within six months. The gates therefore rest on higher-information intermediate evidence (measurement error, nowcast error on first-published values, price transmission) and on mechanism; the held-back window is a consistency check, not a proof.

3System architecture, common to the six ideas

The scaffold is Implemented for data access, point-in-time storage, statistics and validation; the regime, fusion and risk layers are Proposed for I1 and specified below.

Interface contract. Every layer passes, at a timestamp, a value, an uncertainty interval, an age and a coverage flag. A week without an admissible measurement falls back to the public model; uncertainty is propagated, not discarded. Engineering per data type: optical and radar images use physical forward models first (Section 5) and learned models only when labels exist; text uses embeddings with explicit look-ahead controls (filing dates, anonymised identifiers); flows use sequence models; tabular series use linear models or trees.

4I1 specification

4.1 Timeline and hypotheses

EIA stocks are as of Friday 07:00 CT; the industry (API) estimate is published Tuesday 16:30 ET; the EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report is published Wednesday 10:30 ET, shifted in holiday weeks. Release dates come from the EIA report archive (796 releases since 2011, audit/eia_release_registry.csv): the rule calendar used by v1 misdated 14 of 660 weeks in 2011–2024 (audit/calendar_audit.md).

Thesis B, central. Let Yt be the first-published weekly change of Cushing stocks, Xt the public information at a decision time τt before the release (prices, curve, lagged stocks, season, the API figure if already out), and Zt a physical measurement available at τt. B holds if E[ΔPt | Xt, Zt] differs from E[ΔPt | Xt] by more than trading costs, for a tradable price change ΔPt. It is tested as a chain: B-S (does an admissible Z exist, with known error, coverage and age?) → B1 (does Z improve the forecast of Y beyond a public model?) → B2 (does that improvement carry price information?) → B3 (does it survive costs, capacity and the data cost?). A pass at one step proves nothing at the next.
Branch A, complementary. Alturki and Kurov (2022) report that the WTI return in the 30 minutes after the API release predicts the return between 60 minutes and 1 minute before the EIA release (January 2011 – August 2019). A is a replication and a recent extension of a published effect, capped in effort and reported separately. It shares infrastructure with B (events, prices, execution) but is never counted as evidence for B.

Mechanism and counterparty. Participants who react to the print and hedgers without an informational motive. Two forces work against B: storage arbitrage makes stocks respond to prices (a better stock forecast is not automatically a price forecast), and satellite information is partly priced before announcements (Mukherjee, Panayotov and Shon, 2021); vendors already sell measurements. The bar is information beyond prices, not a better picture.

4.2 Targets and surprise proxy

The target is the change of Cushing stocks as first published in the week's report (current minus prior level printed in the same release; results/v2/eia_first_release.csv). Cushing first publications equal the latest values in all 795 archived weeks; US commercial crude differs in 272 weeks of 2011–2016 (audit/vintage_audit.md). The surprise proxy is ut = Yt − m0(Xt), with m0 a recursive AR(2) with intercept on first-published changes since 2011-07-22 (at least 104 rows). It is a proxy, not the market consensus. US crude excluding Cushing is US commercial crude minus Cushing in both terms.

4.4 Instruments and position generation Proposed

  • Instruments. CL outright and the CL1–CL2 calendar spread, in USD per barrel (the traded unit; equal percentage moves of two maturities are not a zero spread move). Products and options only after B2.
  • Decision time. Set by the delivery of the measurement (own pipeline or vendor), before the API release when possible; one primary cut-off chosen from coverage and availability, without looking at returns. A post-API variant is a separate experiment.
  • Signal and abstention. Direction = sign of the forecast price change of the instrument; no position if the forecast edge is below the estimated cost plus a margin fixed on development data. One decision per data week: about 52 a year, fewer without a usable measurement. The outright and the spread are two expressions of one signal, not independent bets.
  • Holding. From the cut-off to six minutes after the print (primary), 30 minutes after (one pre-declared secondary). Branch A has its own window: entry 60 minutes before the EIA release, exit 1 minute before (5 minutes before as a sensitivity).

5B-S: physical measurement from free Sentinel-2 images

Question. Can floating-roof depths at Cushing be measured from 10 m images, repeatably and in time, before any return or EIA error is looked at? Scope. The OpenStreetMap registry holds 333 tanks in the 6.8 × 6.0 km window; the 234 with a diameter of 40 m or more are modelled. Roof types are not tagged: a fixed roof shows no internal shadow and ends as full_or_fixed.

5.1 Forward model Implemented src/altdata/tankshadow.py

When a floating roof sits a depth d below the rim, the wall on the sun side casts a crescent on it of width

s = d / tan θ,  θ = sun elevation.

For each interior pixel p (at least 90% of its 100 sub-points inside the disk), the shadowed share fp(d) is computed on a 1 m sub-grid for d ∈ {0, 0.5, …, 20} m, and the red band is modelled as

xp = a (1 − fp(d)) + b fp(d) + εp,  b ≤ a,

with lit-roof level a and shadow level b re-estimated by least squares for each tank and date, so that atmosphere and calibration drop out. The estimate d̂ minimises the residual sum of squares over the grid; the nominal 68% interval is {d: RSS(d) − RSSmin ≤ σ̂2}. One translation per scene registers the registry to the image (correlation with a synthetic image of all 333 disks, ±2 pixels, parabolic refinement). Abstention, in order: few_pixels (< 8 interior pixels), cloudy (cloud or shadow classes within the disk plus 20 m), no_contrast (a − b < 3σ̂), then kept with a flag: full_or_fixed (d̂ = 0) and wide_interval (> 6 m). The hub proxy is the volume change under the roofs of the tanks measured at both dates, ΔV = −Σi Ai(di,F − di,F−1) × 6.2898 bbl/m3, with Ai the roof area.

Orientation check before any outcome (results/v2/BS1/geometry_check.json): on three winter scenes, the median RSS ratio to a constant fit is 0.153 with the physical sun direction and 0.998 with the mirrored one (701 fits).

5.4 What is and is not established

  • Established: a physically oriented, repeatable roof-depth measurement per tank on low-sun scenes, from free data.
  • Not established: accuracy against operator gauges (none available: repeatability is not accuracy), and a weekly hub variable that tracks EIA.
  • Error model: the nominal intervals are too narrow (33–57% coverage for 68%), so σ̂ understates the error (registration, point-spread function, roof structure). The day-to-day robust spread, 0.74 m, equals 1.4826 times the 0.5 m grid step: the grid, not the method, limits what the stability table can resolve.
  • Hub test: independent tank noise averages out over 234 tanks; scene-wide errors do not. Likely causes of the weak correlation: few Friday-matched passes, up to two days between scene and stock date, scene calibration, tanks outside the EIA scope, and a weekly hub change of only about 1 Mb.
  • Power: detecting r = 0.3 with 80% power at the 5% level needs about 85 weeks; at 14% usable low-sun weeks, that is more than a decade of Sentinel-2. The free optical route cannot settle the hub question quickly; cadence is the constraint.

5.5 Next steps for B-S Proposed

  1. Scene calibration with references inside each scene (fixed roofs, ground), and an empirical error model fitted on repeat pairs instead of σ̂.
  2. Friday anchoring: interpolate depths to the stock date with an interval that grows with the gap.
  3. Sentinel-1 phase data (SLC): roof-height methods from coherent scatterers exist for TerraSAR-X (Villamil Lopez and Stilla, 2021); they are not validated on Sentinel-1 and would be tested as such.
  4. A vendor trial evaluated against the pilot on overlapping weeks (Section 8), and ground truth on a sample: operator gauges or very-high-resolution tasking.

6Price experiments: B0 to B3, and branch A

6.1 B0: transmission at the print Empirical finding Exploratory

Design (scripts/b0_transmission.py, deviations items 3–8). Events: Wednesday 10:30 releases dated by archive folder, 2013-08 to 2023-12 and 2026-06 to 2026-10 (562 candidates; excluded: 81 holiday-shifted, 2 data-only days, 15 without minute data, 3 without a surprise). Outcome: ΔP = close of the 10:35 bar minus close of the 10:29 bar, i.e. the WTI bid change from 10:30:00 to 10:36:00 ET, in USD per barrel. Regressors: the four first-published surprises (Cushing, US ex-Cushing, gasoline, distillate), each the error of its recursive AR(2), in million barrels. OLS with HC1 errors. Rule: transmission detected if the 95% interval of the Cushing coefficient lies below zero.

Validity gate and amendment. The pre-registered clock check (10:30 the largest one-minute move of 10:00–10:59 on at least 70% of days) failed: 205 of 464 days, 44%, although the average profile peaks at 10:30 (24.1 cents against 3–4 cents in the 15 minutes before). An argmax rule fails whenever volatility stays high for several minutes, so it did not test the clock. Before any outcome, AM-B0-1 replaced it by a year-wise gate (the mean profile must peak at 10:30 at more than three times its 10:15–10:29 mean). All years 2013–2023 pass; 2026 fails (10.9 cents against 5.5) and is excluded. B0 is therefore exploratory.

6.3 B2: information beyond prices Proposed

Compare g0(X) and g1(X, a) on the price change of each instrument from the cut-off to six minutes after the print, where a is the B1 correction produced out of fold (never fitted on the period it predicts), and X includes the curve, past returns and, after the API, the post-API move used by A. The test statistic is ρ, the out-of-sample correlation between the forecast and the price change; the cost threshold is

ρ* = k / √(2/π) ≈ 0.025 for k = 0.02 (CL).

Promising if the lower 95% bound of ρ is above zero and the estimate above ρ*; stop if the upper bound is below ρ*; otherwise inconclusive. CL and CL1–CL2 are measured separately; the instrument is chosen on development data, then frozen. With five years of weekly decisions (n ≈ 260) the standard error of ρ is about 0.06, so only large effects can be confirmed; small ones will remain inconclusive, and that will be reported as such.

6.4 B3: net value against the same strategy without the source Proposed

Policy: one unit in the direction of the forecast price change, abstention below cost plus a margin fixed on development data. Counterfactual: same calendar, risk rules and costs with the public model only; the value of the source is the PnL difference, not the total PnL. Fills at bid and ask with sizes, fees, latency, weeks without measurement, size limits. Reported: mean per eligible event and per trade with intervals, drawdown, tail risk, concentration by year, abstention rate, break-even cost. Capacity from depth and impact, then the maximum data budget. Fifty-two announcements do not become 104 independent bets by adding a second instrument.

6.5 Branch A: replication and recent extension Proposed not run

A-R reproduces the published regression (WTI return from EIA −60 to −1 minute on the return in the 30 minutes after the API release; January 2011 – August 2019) from a transcription of the paper's definitions, and explains differences before any extension. It was not run before the deadline: the published tables were not accessed, so a faithful protocol could not be fixed. A-P tests persistence on recent tradable quotes: signal from the 30 minutes after 16:30 ET with a quote convention fixed in advance (CL halts at 17:00 ET, at 17:15 before 21 September 2015), entry at EIA −60 minutes, exit at −1; aggressive one-lot orders after 1 s latency, 10 s stress Assumption. Baselines: no position, recursive mean, calendar-only rule. A positive A is reported separately and never as support for B.

7Validation, decision rules and statistical limits

  • Sample status. Development (seen by the researcher), diagnostic (transition periods), held-back (kept by Borel, never inspected). A period already seen is exploratory, whatever its date.
  • Trial accounting. Every extraction, model and instrument variant is a trial and is logged; selection uses development data only; the selected variant is frozen before the evaluation window is opened.
  • Multiple testing. Families are declared in advance (e.g. Holm across the two T1 markets); exploratory results are labelled and never used as confirmation.
  • Gates. Each gate ends promising, inconclusive or stop, by a rule written before the data. Inconclusive is not a pass: it triggers either more data of a stated kind or a stop.

What would falsify B. No admissible weekly variable with a known error at a usable cadence (B-S); no out-of-sample gain over the public model (B1); a gain in stock forecasts with no price information beyond X (B2); a price effect that does not survive costs, capacity or the data cost (B3). Evaluation protocol Proposed: (1) request the history as delivered, with delivery timestamps, coverage flags and revisions, under a research licence; (2) freeze the evaluation code before receiving the data; (3) run B1c(vendor) against B1b on first-published changes, then B2 and B3 on the same windows; (4) compare with the free pilot on overlapping weeks; (5) apply the economic gate.

Economic gate. Buy if G, the annual incremental net PnL with the source minus without it at a size the book allows, exceeds the data and processing cost on a conservative estimate. Break-even per covered event: cmin = C / (q × 1,000 × κ × 52) USD per barrel, for a cost C, q contracts and coverage κ. Illustrative C = 30,000 USD, q = 10, κ = 0.4 gives about 14 cents a barrel per covered event, above the 3–4 cents a one-sd Cushing surprise is worth on the outright at the print (B0). Value must therefore come from the spread, earlier incorporation or size, each tested first.

9Execution, costs, risk, capacity and capital

  • Execution simulation. Fills at bid and ask with displayed sizes, latency 1 s with a 10 s stress, fees per contract; a missing exit quote never deletes an open trade (pre-declared liquidation rule, cost kept) Assumption.
  • Cost. k = round-trip cost / sd of the move: about one tick plus fees (15–25 USD) against a daily sd of 1,000–1,700 USD per contract gives k ≈ 0.02 Assumption, to be replaced by Borel's costs.
  • Risk. Event risk: size so that a two-sd move over the release window costs a fixed share of the risk budget, with the window measured on minute data (sd 39 cents over the six minutes after 10:30), not taken from daily data; CVaR of the release window as a position cap; volatility targeting; no position across an uncertain release time.
  • Capacity. Assumed not binding at a few M$ of annual risk in CL; to be measured on order-book data Assumption.
  • Capital. Backtest, then an isolated logged paper run, then 25%, 50% and 100% of target risk. Proposed defaults, to be set with Borel's risk team: halve risk at a drawdown of one annual sd of strategy PnL, stop at 1.5. Capital only under Borel's mandate and approval.

Conditional PnL Illustrative. For constant-size bets on the sign of a forecast (Gaussian approximation), with N independent decisions a year and annual risk budget B:

IR = √N (√(2/π) ρ − k),  PnL = IR × B.

For I1 (N = 52, k = 0.02, B = 1 M$): 0.03 / 0.20 / 0.43 M$ a year at ρ = 0.03 / 0.06 / 0.10 (results/v2/pnl_grid_v2.csv, scripts/pnl_grid.py). ρ is the correlation with the price move, measured in B2, not with the stock surprise. The first grid counted 52 oil and 52 gas reports (N = 104); it is kept in results/pnl_grid.csv. These are orders of magnitude conditional on ρ, never results.

11Six-month roadmap Proposed

WhenWorkGate (promising / inconclusive / stop)
Month 1B-S at hub level (Sentinel-1 phase data or a vendor trial against the pilot); B1 against B1b; CL1–CL2 quotes; A capped; I2 in parallel on free dataGate 1: a weekly physical variable that beats the public nowcast out of sample (B-S, B1). Otherwise B stops and I2 is promoted.
Months 2–3B2 on prices, then B3 net of costs and capacity; regime and risk layers only where they change a decision; placebosGate 2: price information above ρ* (B2); incremental net PnL positive on both halves, capacity estimated (B3).
Months 4–6Isolated, logged paper run; next idea prepared; held-back window kept by BorelGate 3: paper gap at most the assumed cost plus 50%; ρ on the held-back window above ρ*; capital only with Borel's approval.

Success at six months: one strategy validated on held-back data with positive net PnL and an estimated capacity, or a documented stop with the gap quantified; a second idea through Gate 1; reproducible code and point-in-time data. Stop at any time: a source that cannot be verified point-in-time; ρ below its threshold; a fusion model that does not beat the baseline; a data trial whose incremental value is below its cost; the drawdown rules.
